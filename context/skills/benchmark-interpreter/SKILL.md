# AP Benchmark Interpreter

Take a metric name and a client value, place it against the peer points, and say in plain English what that position means. The discipline here is that a number without its comparison points and its unit is not a finding, and several of these metrics are actively misleading when read on their own.

## Read this first

`sample-data/ap-benchmark-data.csv` is authoritative, with columns `field_id`, `metric`, `unit`, `global_benchmark`, `india_benchmark`, `commonly_observed`, `read`. Read it at the start of every run. The table below mirrors it for reference. If the two disagree, the file wins and you should say so.

## The ten benchmark rows

| field_id | Metric | Unit | Global | India | Commonly observed | Better direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ap_derived | Invoices processed per AP FTE | thousands p.a. | 44 | 40 | 12-25 | higher |
| ap_04 | PO-backed invoices | % | 95 | 85 | 70-90 | higher |
| ap_08 | Invoice exception rate | % | 2 | 8 | 10-20 | lower |
| ap_09 | Invoice receipt to posting | days | 2 | 4 | 3-8 | lower |
| ap_10 | Days Payable Outstanding | days | 45 | 55 | 40-70 | neither, see below |
| ap_11 | Paid on time against terms | % | 95 | 70 | 20-50 | higher |
| ap_12 | Payments made electronically | % | 98 | 75 | 5-40 | higher |
| ap_13 | Distinct payment terms in use | count | 8 | 20 | 30-80 | lower |
| ap_14 | Early payment discount capture | % | 85 | 45 | under 50 | higher |
| ap_17 | Vendor queries per 1000 invoices | count | 15 | 40 | 50-120 | lower |

The "better direction" column is not in the file. It is the interpretation layer, and without it a position gets described backwards: 18 percent exceptions against an India point of 8 is *worse*, not ahead.

## Name resolution

Resolve common variants to the rows above: DPO, days payable, payable days to `ap_10`. On-time payment, OTP, payment timeliness to `ap_11`. PO coverage, PO compliance, non-PO share (invert it and say you inverted it) to `ap_04`. Exception rate, rework rate, fallout rate to `ap_08`. Cycle time, touch time, receipt to post to `ap_09`. Discount capture, early payment discount, cash discount to `ap_14`. Productivity, invoices per head, per-FTE throughput to `ap_derived`.

If a metric does not resolve to one of the ten, say so plainly, list what the file does cover, and stop. Do not estimate a benchmark. The file has **no** benchmark for GR-IR ageing, match tolerance, vendor count, duplicate rate, onboarding time, OCR hit rate, approval step count, payment run frequency, channel mix or MSME exposure. Those are genuinely absent, not something to fill in.

## Metrics that cannot be read alone

Refuse the read and name what is needed:

- **DPO (`ap_10`) needs paid-on-time (`ap_11`).** This is the important one. A DPO near the India point of 55 days looks like competent working capital management and is very often late payment wearing a treasury costume. With low paid-on-time, high DPO means the client is funding itself on its vendors by accident. Say "this needs paid-on-time to interpret" rather than giving a read.
- **Exception rate (`ap_08`) needs PO coverage (`ap_04`).** Exceptions are usually inherited from low coverage or a tight match tolerance rather than generated by vendor error. Without coverage you cannot say whose problem it is.
- **Invoices per FTE (`ap_derived`) needs `ap_03` and `ap_19` to compute, and `ap_04` to compare.** The file is explicit: compare only after normalising for non-PO mix. A low figure with a high non-PO share is a process problem, not a people problem, and quoting it raw reads as a criticism of the team.
- **Vendor queries (`ap_17`) needs invoice volume (`ap_03`) to normalise.** The field is collected as a monthly count and the benchmark is per 1000 invoices. Convert, and show the arithmetic.
- **Paid on time (`ap_11`) is hard to manage while terms count (`ap_13`) is high.** A long tail of terms makes the on-time figure difficult to measure honestly. Give the read, but add that caveat when `ap_13` is above the India point of 20.

## Output shape, per metric

1. **The three points, with the unit on every one.** Global, India, client. Add the commonly observed band, since a client sitting inside it is normal for the market even when both peer points look far away.
2. **Position, stated as a fact.** Ahead of, near, or behind the India point. Use the better-direction column so the wording is the right way round. "Near" means within roughly ten percent of the point for a percentage or count, or within a day for a days metric.
3. **One line on what it means.** Use the file's `read` column as the source of the reasoning, reworded for a client rather than quoted verbatim.
4. **A dependency note where one applies**, naming the missing metric.

Where several metrics are passed at once, do them in causal order rather than the order given: coverage, then exceptions, then cycle time, then on-time payment, then discount capture. The sequence is the argument.

## Rules this skill must enforce

- Never report a number without its unit.
- Never quote a benchmark that is not in the file, and never interpolate one.
- Never give a DPO read without paid-on-time alongside it.
- Never describe a position without naming both peer points.
- Do not call the client's own figure good or bad in isolation. State the position, then the meaning.
- If the client value looks outside the commonly observed band by a wide margin, say the figure is worth confirming before it goes in a report. A typo in an input becomes a wrong finding.
- Plain language, no jargon. A client's finance team reads this, not a data analyst.
